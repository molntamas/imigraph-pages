    WHAT THIS IS

    MAP is Conway's Game of Life: a cell is born with exactly 3 alive neighbours, stays alive with 2 or 3, and dies otherwise. Colours: just born, young, old, and a fading trail where a cell died.

    GRAPH shows the same world as a hypergraph, the kind of structure the Wolfram Physics Project builds worlds from: nothing but nodes and relations between them. Every cell is a node. Each node carries 20 relations: one that names it with its eight neighbours, nine that put those neighbours in an order, one that says alive ({c}, drawn as a ring) or dead ({c,c,c,c,c}), one that says it is ready, and eight lines to its neighbours. Zoom in to see them.

    WOLFRAM draws one cell the way the project draws a hypergraph. Choose the cell with PROBE.

    How the two belong together: a Wolfram model of 17 rewriting rules plays Life on exactly this hypergraph, cell for cell. This game turns that round. It computes with the ordinary rule, which is far faster, and writes down the hypergraph that the 17 rules would hold at that moment. Tests hold the two equal, relation for relation, between every two generations. What you do not see here are the ten small steps in which the rules work out one generation.
